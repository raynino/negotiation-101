# DAV Patriot Boot Camp
## Term Sheet Negotiation Case File – BUYER ONLY

### Client Background

**Lucerne:** Rocca Holdings, Inc. (“Rocca”) is the corporate entity that operates the Lucerne chain of outdoor equipment stores. Lucerne is a strict retailer; it does not offer guided fishing trips, classes, or other add-on services typically found in smaller outfitters. Based in the Western U.S., Lucerne has focused on providing a “middle high” shopping experience, competitive prices, and more selection for sports outside of the bread-and-butter pursuits of the American outdoorsman (hunting and fishing). Its wider variety of skiing, climbing, and mountaineering equipment has helped it carve out a lucrative niche.

**Strategy:** Jordan Rocca is not satisfied with the execution of the expansion strategy thus far and has pushed to be more aggressive in the acquisition of smaller stores. Paired with a kindred spirit in Robin Brozost, the two are committed to establishing a foothold in the Cincinnati market by acquiring Evans Outfitters.

### Negotiating Instructions

**Authority:** You have the freedom to negotiate every aspect of the Term Sheet and add terms you deem advisable. Some pushback from Evans Outfitters is expected. The guidance below should assist you with respect to the parameters for Rocca.

**Price:** Although profitability has declined slightly over the past few years, owner compensation has increased. Rocca is also confident economies of scale will reduce some expenses. Rocca is paying up to 3X average net profit (roughly $2.7 million) but obviously would prefer to pay less if possible.

**Earnout:** Rocca would prefer if some of the purchase price is contingent on the performance of the business over the first year following transaction. For example, a deal structured as $1 million at closing, $1 million a year later, and all profit in excess of $750,000 over the first year of ownership would be palatable.

**Payment:** Rocca has taken on some debt to fund the expansion and thus would like to avoid financing the entire acquisition. Stretching the purchase price payments over three years would help their cash position significantly.

**Owners:** Rocca wishes to employ Ashley and Kelly for one year following acquisition at slightly reduced salary levels, subject to termination for cause and a two-year non-compete.

**Exclusivity:** Rocca would like a 120-day, exclusive negotiating period to arrive at a final deal. During that time, EO cannot market the business to any third parties.

**Diligence:** During the negotiating period, EO will produce all of its financial and business information for review by Rocca, including financial statements, monthly sales figures, available inventory, employment files, etc.

### General Notes

**Price:** The purchase price formula is a general rule of thumb and is not written in stone. Whether 2.5X owner compensation or 3X net profit, the upper limit for Rocca is approximately $2.7 million. You are free to go over that amount, but a more expensive transaction should reflect lowered risk.

**Owners:** Locking up the owners as full time employees for one year, subject to stringent for-cause and non-compete provisions, is important to Rocca. They need involved owners for the first year to ensure a smooth transition. If they are not amenable to continuing in their present duties, Rocca would agree to a consulting contract or some other more limited relationship that requires a minimum of 30 hours per week. Ashley is also viewed by Rocca as more important than Kelly in terms of retention.

**Exclusivity:** Exclusivity is critical; you do not want Evans or Guerin shopping the Rocca bid around. This could drive up the price if other suitors enter the field, could affect the other expansion activities Rocca is conducting, and would put the entire transaction on a slower track. Length is flexible, though should not dip below 45 days.

**NDA:** The Term Sheet may contain a confidential provision or incorporate a Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA). These are usually relatively standard, but Rocca does not want to grant liquidated damages or some other unusual remedies.

**KY:** Brozost is not sure that the Kentucky location has the same value as the Mason and Hyde Park locations. Evans Outfitters may have expanded too quickly. Closing the Kentucky location might be the right move and should obviously reduce the purchase price.

**Trips:** Rocca has no interest in the “excursion” adventure trips offered by Evans Outfitters, despite its success. This also can be used as leverage to lower the purchase price.

**Clothing Line:** Brozost is encouraged by the numbers, marketing, and buzz surrounding the EO proprietary clothing line. Though such products often do not scale well, the EO brand is high quality, and its sales performance is impressive. Securing this asset is a major priority for the transaction.
