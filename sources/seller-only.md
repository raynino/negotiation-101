# DAV Patriot Boot Camp
## Term Sheet Negotiation Case File – SELLER ONLY

### Client Background

**Origin:** Evans Outfitters, LLC (“EO” or “Company”) was formed in 2015 by Ashley Evans and Terry Guerin. Ashley Evans graduated from Miami University in 2004 with a degree in finance and immediately worked for REI, Inc. at its Seattle headquarters. Ashley’s primary duties at REI included forecasting revenue, analyzing sales and demographic trends, and translating quantitative data for use in retail and product development. When Ashley’s father died suddenly in 2009, Ashley returned to Cincinnati to be near Ashley’s mom and younger brother, both of whom live in the area. Terry Guerin grew up in Chicago and graduated from the University of Cincinnati in 2008. A star student in the DAAP design program, Terry worked remotely for an international brand design agency for several years, traveling frequently to New York, Europe, and Asia. After starting a family in 2014, Terry sought an opportunity closer to home. Friends with Ashley through a mutual acquaintance, the two decided to launch Evans Outfitters.

**Strategy:** After a decade of success, both Ashley and Terry are accustomed to overtures from salespersons, whether offering IT services, retail items, or acquisition offers. They have found it valuable to entertain such conversations to gain insight into the larger industry and gauge the value of EO in the market. Ashley and Terry are committed to selling the business in the next two years.

### Negotiating Instructions

**Authority:** You have complete authority to negotiate the transaction, following the parameters outlined in this document.

**Price:** The ultimate goal is a price equal to 4X annual net profit, or $3.2 million. They are willing to accept to 80% of this number if other aspects of the deal are favorable. (REI offered to buy the company a few years ago for roughly $2 million). Both Ashley and Terry are opposed to an earnout unless the upfront closing payment is a significant percentage of overall deal value.

**Payment:** The owners would obviously prefer all of the purchase price at closing. They are willing to accept half at closing with the remainder in six months. Beyond that would be difficult to live with.

**Exclusivity:** A short window of exclusivity, such as 30 days, is preferable to maintain deal momentum. Beyond 60 days, EO would prefer the freedom to entertain other offers. EO is unwilling to grant due diligence access beyond the exclusivity period.

### General Notes

**Confidential:** The Company’s major concern with exploring a transaction is the access that Rocca would have to sensitive, confidential information. If a deal is not ultimately concluded, the confidential information gained via due diligence could be used to compete against EO, both in Cincinnati and potentially in other markets. Kelly is particularly concerned about Rocca hiring away some of their star employees. EO wants very strict confidentiality protections backed by severe liquidated damages, something like $100,000 for each breach.

**Break-Up:** Ashley has suggested adding a break-up fee to the Term Sheet that requires Rocca to pay EO $50,000 and all legal fees if a deal is not consummated.

**Financials:** The financial statements are prepared in-house; Ashley works with the Company’s bookkeeper to compile the relevant information. Ashley has a sharp financial mind and is able to process the Company’s financials on a week-to-week basis. Both owners run a number of personal expenses through the business, including vehicles, clothing, and “excursion research” trips that are actually family vacations.

**Clothing Line:** The Evans Outfitters clothing line has done much better than they ever expected. Kelly and Ashley designed the apparel themselves, engaged a branding expert, established relationships with a manufacturer, and set up a distribution network. A lower purchase price is acceptable if they can retain ownership of the clothing line and license it back to Rocca for a reasonable price.

**Litigation:** EO is currently a defendant in a lawsuit filed by a former employee who alleges age discrimination. Ashley is confident the suit lacks merit, but settlement negotiations have not been fruitful.

**Locations:** The Kentucky retail location is the least profitable store out of the three Evans locations. It represents about 18.5% of merchandise sales for Evans and only about 12% of the excursion sales. Mason and Rookwood are about equal.

**Employment:** Ashley and Kelly both lack the desire to continue working for Rocca following the sale, though they are aware that continued employment is likely a requirement of the transaction. Both would prefer to limit such employment to six months and no more than 30 hours per week if feasible.
